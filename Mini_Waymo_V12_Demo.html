The challenge

After presenting Opera and emerging technology to our class, guest speaker Dan Berte challenged us to build something that appears to think—in just three weeks.

We chose an autonomous miniature Waymo: a playful way to put real sensing, control, and engineering tradeoffs into something barely bigger than a toy.

What we're building

A self-contained diecast car that drives between room doors, stops for obstacles, works out where it is, and reports its decisions. The drivetrain comes from a 1/43 RC drift car inside the Waymo shell, so it steers like a real car.

Two versions: V1, a line follower on floor tape, is the fallback. V2, our version, sweeps a small depth sensor on a roof turret at each stop to match the hallway to the saved map, and reads room markers with a camera behind the windshield.

Our goal is an indoor, supervised demonstration, not a self-driving car on public roads. Parts and progress on the Build page ↗

Powered by a home server

Privately run, not university infrastructure

The website, the map, the task board and the car's mission link run on a private home server owned by a team member. The server plans trips and keeps history; the car keeps every immediate safety decision onboard, so it stops on its own if the link drops.
